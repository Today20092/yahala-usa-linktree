## Recommendation in one sentence

Build a concise, first-person `/about` page that answers **who Haithum is, what Ya Hala does, why he does it, and what a visitor should do next**, led by a real headshot and grounded in a few representative stories rather than a long résumé.

The project's public spelling is **Haithum**. His professional profile uses **Haithum Alqahaf**, while an official Institute of Management Accountants announcement uses **Mhd Haithum Alqahaf**. Confirm his preferred full display name, Arabic spelling, and pronunciation before writing final copy.

## What the page should accomplish

In priority order:

1. **Recognition:** immediately show the human host behind Ya Hala.
2. **Orientation:** explain the show in one plain sentence.
3. **Connection:** let Haithum state why these stories matter to him.
4. **Proof:** show two or three representative field stories, communities, or moments.
5. **Action:** offer one primary next step—watch the latest/featured story—and one secondary step—follow Ya Hala.

This is a value page, not a résumé page. The visitor's payoff is understanding whose perspective shapes the show and why the work is worth following.

## Recommended content and order

### 1. Hero: identity and promise

- A current, professionally lit but natural head-and-shoulders photograph of Haithum.
- H1: **About Haithum Alqahaf** (pending his preferred spelling).
- One-line role: **Host and creator of Ya Hala with Haithum** (confirm “creator” before publishing).
- A 25–40 word value statement in his voice: who he meets, what he helps audiences discover, and why that matters.
- Primary CTA: **Watch a featured story**.

Do not use the channel logo as the person image. Google advises that a profile image should represent the person and should not be a default image, icon, or placeholder ([Google ProfilePage guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)).

### 2. “Who I am”

Use two short first-person paragraphs:

- Paragraph one: personal background and relationship to Arab American communities.
- Paragraph two: the path from his earlier professional life into interviewing and field storytelling.

Only the limited finance/accounting background above is currently verified. Every other sentence in this section should come from Haithum's interview and approval.

### 3. “Why I started Ya Hala”

This is the emotional center of the page. Ask for:

- the moment or problem that prompted the show;
- whose stories he felt were missing or misunderstood;
- what “Ya Hala” means to him;
- what he hopes an Arab viewer and a non-Arab viewer each carry away;
- the principle that guides whom he visits and how he tells their stories.

Prefer a specific origin scene over generic claims such as “I have always been passionate about storytelling.”

### 4. “What you’ll find here”

Three compact themes are enough:

- people and their journeys;
- community places, businesses, and organizations;
- life and culture across Arab America.

Each theme should link to a real representative video or existing browse/search experience. Avoid a new content taxonomy until the existing catalog proves one is needed.

### 5. Selected stories

Show two or three editorially chosen episodes with thumbnail, descriptive title, location/person, and a short sentence explaining why each represents the show's purpose. These should be chosen by Haithum, not automatically ranked by views.

### 6. Compact credentials and transparency

If Haithum wants his prior career included, keep it to a short fact strip or sentence rather than a chronology. A CMA credential may be included only after he confirms that it remains current and wants it public; the issuing organization's announcement verifies that he became a CMA in March 2020, not current standing ([IMA/Strategic Finance announcement](https://www.sfmagazine.com/articles/2020/may/welcome-new-cmas-march-2020)).

Add a clear business/contact path only if an official public address or form is supplied. Do not surface personal phone numbers, home addresses, or brokered contact data.

### 7. Closing invitation

End with a warm sentence in Haithum's voice and two choices:

- **Watch Ya Hala**
- **Follow the journey**

If community members can propose a story, that is a useful third action only when a real intake channel and response owner exist.

## UX, accessibility, and search requirements

- Use one informative page title, such as **About Haithum Alqahaf | Ya Hala**, with the unique page name first. W3C recommends short, distinguishing page titles with the most relevant information first ([W3C writing guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/)).
- Use one H1 and descriptive H2 sections without skipped heading levels. Headings aid scanning and assistive-technology navigation ([W3C heading guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/page-structure/headings/)).
- Give the headshot meaningful alt text only for information not already conveyed by adjacent copy; decorative imagery should have empty alt text. W3C requires text alternatives to communicate an image's information or function ([W3C writing guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/)).
- Use descriptive CTA labels such as “Watch Haithum's story from Dearborn,” not “Learn more” or “Click here.” W3C recommends link text that describes its destination ([W3C writing guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/)).
- Keep paragraphs short and copy concrete. W3C recommends clear, concise content and headings for documents longer than three or four paragraphs ([W3C writing guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tips/writing/)).
- Add `ProfilePage` JSON-LD whose `mainEntity` is a `Person`, with verified `name`, `description`, `image`, and `sameAs` links. Google explicitly recognizes an About Me page as a valid profile-page use and recommends those Person fields ([Google ProfilePage guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)).
- Include a crawlable high-resolution headshot. Google recommends profile imagery that represents the marked-up person and, for best results, multiple high-resolution 1:1, 4:3, and 16:9 variants ([Google ProfilePage guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)).
- Add `/about` to navigation, internal links, and the sitemap. Validate structured data with Google's Rich Results Test and inspect the deployed URL in Search Console ([Google ProfilePage guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/profile-page)).
- Preserve the site's existing mobile-first layout, keyboard focus treatment, color tokens, reduced-motion behavior, and fast static rendering. No carousel, biography timeline, testimonial system, or new dependency is needed for the first version.

## Headshot brief

Request one original photo session/export with:

- authentic expression and direct or near-direct eye contact;
- uncluttered setting related to the show, or a quiet neutral background;
- enough space around the face for 1:1, 4:3, and 16:9 crops;
- high-resolution original plus optimized WebP/AVIF derivatives;
- confirmation that Haithum owns or has licensed the image for web, social, and press use;
- no embedded text, heavy filters, or AI-generated likeness.

Also request one optional working image of Haithum interviewing someone in the field. The hero should still use the clear headshot.

14. Is there an approved public email, contact form, press kit, or story-submission path?
15. May the site publish the selected headshot, field photo, and Arabic biography?
